How It Works: Marketing Analytics Software That Shows What Every Campaign Made

Most marketing analytics software stops at the dashboard: clicks, leads, and which channel gets credit. CDAI (Capital, Decision, Accuracy, Intelligence), the engine behind Allocera Intelligence, goes one step further. It connects your ad accounts and CRM, works out what each campaign really made after every cost, and gives you one clear decision for every campaign, every night. Here is exactly how, in four steps.

The gap

Where most marketing analytics software stops

Most marketing analytics software collects data from your ad platforms, CRM, and web analytics into one view. That is genuinely useful. Seeing every channel together beats logging in to five dashboards.

But for a business that runs on leads, the number that decides everything usually isn’t in any of those dashboards. A campaign’s leads may be bought through a partner who takes a payout. Some customers refund. Some payments are charged back weeks later. Compliance and fulfillment cost money on every sale. None of that lives in the ad account, so the dashboard can show a healthy return on a campaign that is losing money. Our guide to marketing costs walks through each cost the platforms can’t see.

CDAI was built for that gap. It’s marketing analytics software for lead generation that answers the question the owner actually asks: did this campaign make money, and what should I do with it tomorrow?

CDAI doesn’t compete with your CRM or call tracking. It sits above them and reconciles everything into one number per campaign, then tells you what to do.

The process

How CDAI works in 4 steps

  1. Connect. Link the ad accounts, CRM, and other tools you already use. Nothing about your campaigns has to change.
  2. Calculate. Every night, CDAI pulls fresh data and works out real profit, true cost per lead, and true customer acquisition cost for every campaign.
  3. Decide. For each campaign, it makes one clear call, with the numbers and a confidence level behind it.
  4. Grade. It rechecks its own math and, once enough time has passed, grades whether each past decision was right.

What happens every night, in this order

  1. Ingest. CDAI pulls in your data from the platforms you connected. It is built for 10 platforms, plus CSV import and a generic webhook, and it reads a rolling window matched to your sales cycle (30 days by default).
  2. Build each campaign’s profit and loss. Ad spend, fees, partner payouts, refunds, chargebacks, compliance costs, and operating costs you add, against the revenue it closed.
  3. Check for risk. It watches for rising cost per lead, lead-quality decay, fraud concentrating in a campaign, chargeback spikes, shrinking margins, and partner payouts that eat too much of the revenue.
  4. Project the close-rate trend. It tracks each campaign’s weekly close rate and projects it forward, so a collapse is flagged before it reaches your margin.
  5. Make the decision. One of eight for each campaign: Scale, Hold, Cut, Pause, Quarantine, Renegotiate, Investigate, or Flag.
  6. Gate it. Every decision carries a confidence level, thin data can’t produce a Scale, and a stability gate stops decisions from flip-flopping night to night.
  7. Grade past decisions. Once a sales cycle has passed, CDAI scores whether each earlier decision was right, with the dollar impact.
  8. Lock attribution at first touch. A lead’s campaign doesn’t drift on re-sync, and revenue that can’t be tied to a campaign is kept separate, never forced into one.
  9. Run data-health checks. Stale or broken feeds are caught before they skew a decision.
  10. Update the partner scorecard. Close rate, payouts, refunds, chargebacks, and a risk tier for every lead partner.
  11. Update the budget model. An estimate of how much spend could move from Cut campaigns to Scale campaigns. It is an estimate, not a forecast.
  12. Send the nightly digest. The decisions arrive in your email as well as your dashboard.

The math

What CDAI calculates

This is the part most marketing analytics software skips. For every campaign, every night, CDAI calculates real profit:

Real profit = revenue closed − ad spend − fees − partner payouts − refunds − chargebacks − compliance costs − operating costs you add

From the same data it also works out true cost per lead, true customer acquisition cost, and margin per campaign.

The nightly decision

One clear decision for every campaign

Numbers alone don’t move budget, and that’s where most marketing analytics software leaves you on your own. So after calculating, CDAI makes one clear decision for each campaign, chosen from eight:

ScaleThe campaign is profitable, has no risk signals, and has enough data behind it. Add budget.
HoldThe default. Keep the budget steady. This also covers campaigns whose deals haven’t closed yet, or where there isn’t enough data to confirm a Scale.
CutThe campaign is losing money on real closed revenue. Reduce or stop spend. CDAI won’t cut a campaign that still has open pipeline just because deals haven’t closed.
PauseAn emergency stop for extreme fraud, severe losses, or a chargeback surge.
QuarantineFraud is concentrating in this campaign’s leads. Isolate it and check the source.
RenegotiateA partner’s payouts are taking too much of the revenue. Renegotiate the terms.
InvestigateLead quality is slipping, or the close rate is projected to collapse. Review the partner or traffic source.
FlagThe data doesn’t add up, so a person checks before anyone acts.

Each decision comes with the numbers behind it and a confidence level. CDAI also watches for early warning signs, like true cost per lead rising faster than the dashboard’s, or close rates slipping, so problems show up before they reach your books.

You stay in control of every move. CDAI recommends, and you decide what changes in your ad accounts. Cut, Pause, and Quarantine are advisory like every other decision: CDAI never changes your ad accounts, and a person acts on the call. Proof

How CDAI checks itself

Common questions

Marketing analytics software FAQs

What does marketing analytics software do?
It collects data from your ad platforms, CRM, and web analytics into one place, so you can see how campaigns perform across channels. CDAI does that too, then goes further: it works out real profit for every campaign after every cost and gives you one clear decision for each, every night.
What are the four main types of marketing analytics?
The four types most often described are descriptive (what happened), diagnostic (why it happened), predictive (what may happen next), and prescriptive (what to do about it). CDAI sits mostly in the prescriptive group: it tells you what to do. Its decisions come from fixed, published rules applied to your numbers, not from a black-box model.
Does CDAI change my ads automatically?
No. CDAI recommends and you decide. Every decision, including Cut, Pause, and Quarantine, is advisory. CDAI never changes your ad accounts, and a person acts on the call.
What if a tool I use isn’t one of the built integrations?
You can upload a CSV export (nine common platform formats are recognized automatically) or send leads from any other CRM or form tool through the webhook.
Do I need to connect everything before it works?
No. Start with your ad accounts and CRM. CDAI works with what you connect, and each added source makes every campaign’s number more complete. Your CRM matters most, because that is where closed deals live.
How accurate are CDAI’s decisions?
Accuracy is measured per decision type, never as one overall number. In the September 2026 validation (1,352 scored decisions across 9 simulated businesses in 7 industries), Scale was 98.0%, Hold 95.2%, Pause 85.2%, and Flag 70.9%. Cut was 55.6% on a very small sample, and every miss came from one deliberately extreme stress-test business. The full table is above, and the validation report publishes every result.
