<h2 id="what">What Is Salesforce Campaign Influence?</h2>
<p>Salesforce Campaign Influence connects your marketing campaigns to the opportunities they helped create. On <a href="https://trailhead.salesforce.com/trailblazer-community/feed/0D54S00000A9AZwSAN" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Salesforce&rsquo;s Trailblazer Community</a>, it&rsquo;s described as a tool that helps you attribute a percentage of success to influential campaigns, with two versions available: Campaign Influence 1.0 and Customizable Campaign Influence.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Campaign Influence 1.0</strong> is the original version, used in older setups.</li>
  <li><strong>Customizable Campaign Influence</strong> supports more than one attribution model, so you can decide how revenue credit is split across the campaigns that touched a deal.</li>
</ul>
<p>The simplest model gives all the credit to one campaign, the opportunity&rsquo;s Primary Campaign Source. Custom models spread the credit across several campaigns. Either way, the output is the same kind of number: how much won revenue each campaign gets credit for.</p>
<p>One practical warning if you switch versions: a <a href="https://trailhead.salesforce.com/trailblazer-community/feed/0D54S00000A8yzTSAR" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Trailblazer Community answer</a> notes that influence records created in Campaign Influence 1.0 won&rsquo;t show up once Customizable Campaign Influence is enabled.</p>

<h2 id="how">How Salesforce Campaign Influence Works in Practice</h2>
<p>Salesforce Campaign Influence depends on a chain of records being filled in correctly:</p>
<ol class="sfc-steps">
  <li><strong>A person becomes a campaign member.</strong> A lead or contact is added to a campaign, by a form, an import, a marketing tool, or by hand.</li>
  <li><strong>That person is added to an opportunity as a contact role.</strong> Auto-association, as another <a href="https://trailhead.salesforce.com/trailblazer-community/feed/0D54S00000A9CRaSAN" target="_blank" rel="noopener">community answer</a> explains, relies on campaign members being assigned as a contact role on an opportunity.</li>
  <li><strong>Salesforce creates influence records</strong> linking the opportunity to the campaigns that person was a member of, and splits the credit by your model.</li>
  <li><strong>When the opportunity closes,</strong> the won amount flows to the campaigns in proportion to their credit.</li>
</ol>
<p>If a step is skipped (a lead converted without a contact role, a caller never added to a campaign), the campaign gets no credit. That&rsquo;s the most common reason Salesforce Campaign Influence reports look thin: it&rsquo;s usually a data-entry gap, not a Salesforce bug.</p>

<h2 id="roi">How Salesforce Calculates Campaign ROI</h2>
<p>Salesforce includes a Campaign ROI Analysis report. <a href="https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=analytics.reports_campaign.htm&amp;language=en&amp;type=5" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Salesforce Help</a> says it calculates the return on investment and average costs for your campaigns, with ROI calculated as the net gain divided by the campaign&rsquo;s cost. In practice:</p>
<p><strong>Campaign ROI = (value of won opportunities &minus; Actual Cost) &divide; Actual Cost</strong></p>
<p>The cost side comes from the campaign&rsquo;s Actual Cost field. Salesforce&rsquo;s <a href="https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=000381471&amp;language=en_US&amp;type=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener">own guidance</a> is to fill out the Actual Cost field when the campaign is completed, and that value is pulled into the ROI report.</p>
<p>That&rsquo;s a clean, simple calculation, and it pairs naturally with Salesforce Campaign Influence for the revenue side. The question is what goes into it.</p>

<h2 id="gaps">4 Things Salesforce Campaign ROI Misses</h2>
<p>None of these are flaws in Salesforce. They&rsquo;re the edges of what a CRM field and a won amount can hold. But each one pushes the ROI number away from real profit.</p>
<h3>1. Costs live in one manual field</h3>
<p>Actual Cost is a single number. As <a href="https://www.revopsglobal.com/tracking-campaign-roi-in-salesforce-solving-the-cost-visibility-problem/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">RevOps Global</a> puts it, it&rsquo;s just a single field, and it must be manually updated by the marketing team. A <a href="https://trailhead.salesforce.com/trailblazer-community/feed/0D5KX00000anHOW0A2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Trailblazer Community answer</a> adds that it doesn&rsquo;t automatically add up custom expense fields. If nobody updates it after this month&rsquo;s ad invoices, the ROI is stale.</p>
<h3>2. A won amount isn&rsquo;t money kept</h3>
<p>An opportunity closed at $10,000 stays $10,000 in the report, even if part of it is refunded, cancelled, or charged back weeks later.</p>
<h3>3. Costs of the sale sit outside the campaign</h3>
<p>Lead vendor and partner payouts, card processing and financing fees, and the cost to deliver the work rarely make it into Actual Cost. They&rsquo;re real costs of each sale the campaign produced. Our guide to <a href="https://alloceraintelligence.com/marketing-costs/">marketing costs</a> lists them all.</p>
<h3>4. Credit isn&rsquo;t the same as contribution</h3>
<p>Salesforce Campaign Influence decides how to split revenue credit. It doesn&rsquo;t tell you whether a campaign&rsquo;s share of revenue covered that campaign&rsquo;s share of costs. Two campaigns with the same influenced revenue can have very different profit.</p>

<h2 id="fix">How to Get Closer to Profit Inside Salesforce</h2>
<p>If you want Salesforce Campaign Influence and campaign ROI to be as close to profit as possible, these habits help:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Update Actual Cost on a schedule.</strong> Monthly at minimum, from the real ad invoices, not the budget.</li>
  <li><strong>Add every cost of the sale.</strong> Include lead vendor payouts and agency fees in the campaign&rsquo;s cost, or track them in custom fields and a formula that sums them (remember Actual Cost won&rsquo;t add them up for you).</li>
  <li><strong>Correct won amounts for refunds.</strong> Reduce the opportunity amount, or record refunds against the campaign, when money comes back.</li>
  <li><strong>Enforce contact roles.</strong> No contact role, no influence. Make it part of your opportunity process.</li>
  <li><strong>Pick one influence model and keep it.</strong> Changing models changes the history, so compare like with like.</li>
  <li><strong>Wait for the sales cycle.</strong> Judge a campaign after its deals have had time to close; our <a href="https://alloceraintelligence.com/30-day-retest-methodology/">30-day retest</a> explains why the window matters.</li>
</ul>
<p>This works, but it&rsquo;s manual, and it gets harder as campaigns, partners, and payment tools multiply. Our guide to <a href="https://alloceraintelligence.com/calculate-contribution-margin/">calculating contribution margin</a> shows the full method in a spreadsheet.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Salesforce Campaign Influence FAQs</h2>
<details>
  <summary>What is Salesforce Campaign Influence?</summary>
  <p>Salesforce Campaign Influence is a feature that links campaigns to the opportunities they helped create and splits credit for the revenue between them. It comes in two versions: Campaign Influence 1.0 and Customizable Campaign Influence.</p>
</details>
<details>
  <summary>Why isn&rsquo;t Campaign Influence showing on my opportunities?</summary>
  <p>The most common reason is a missing contact role. Auto-association relies on campaign members being added as contact roles on the opportunity. If the person who responded to the campaign isn&rsquo;t a contact role, the campaign gets no influence.</p>
</details>
<details>
  <summary>How does Salesforce calculate campaign ROI?</summary>
  <p>The Campaign ROI Analysis report divides the net gain (the value of won opportunities minus the campaign&rsquo;s Actual Cost) by the Actual Cost. Salesforce recommends filling in Actual Cost when the campaign is completed.</p>
</details>
<details>
  <summary>Does Salesforce include ad spend in campaign ROI automatically?</summary>
  <p>Campaign ROI uses the Actual Cost field on the campaign, which is typically entered or updated by the marketing team. If your ad spend, payouts, or fees aren&rsquo;t in that field, they aren&rsquo;t in the ROI.</p>
</details>
<details>
  <summary>What&rsquo;s the difference between Campaign Influence 1.0 and Customizable Campaign Influence?</summary>
  <p>Customizable Campaign Influence supports multiple attribution models for splitting revenue credit, while 1.0 is the original, simpler version. Influence records from 1.0 don&rsquo;t carry over when you enable the customizable version.</p>
</details>
<details>
  <summary>Is Allocera a replacement for Salesforce?</summary>
  <p>No. Salesforce stays your CRM. Allocera connects to it and to your ad accounts, calculates real profit for each campaign every night, and gives you one clear decision per campaign.</p>
</details>
